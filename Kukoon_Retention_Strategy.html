80.8% of customers never returned
Of 176,989 customers who placed at least one order, 143,078 never came back. Improving repeat rate from 19.2% to the 25–30% industry benchmark adds over £1.5M from existing customers alone.
📅
54% of repeat buyers return within 90 days
The repeat purchase window is well-defined. More than half of all customers who will ever buy again do so within 90 days. This makes days 7, 30, 60, and 90 the most critical window for any retention activity.
🇮🇪
Ireland customers are 55% more likely to repeat
Irish customers repeat at 23.7% vs 15.3% for UK — and are 92% more likely to become loyal 3×+ buyers. Retention activity targeting Ireland should be prioritised and adapted for the market, not treated identically to UK.

The core calculation

You have 176,989 customers. If your repeat rate moved from 19.2% to the industry benchmark of 27%, that's an additional 13,987 repeat customers. At an average second-order value of £117, that's £1.63M in incremental revenue — from customers you already acquired, at near-zero marginal cost.

By the 6th purchase a customer is more likely to buy again than not. The flywheel effect is real and consistent — every purchase compounds the next.
🎯
The 1→2 step is the only one that needs heavy investment
At 18.6%, the first repeat is the hardest. Every subsequent step is progressively easier — 2→3 is 27%, 3→4 is 34%. The implication is clear: concentrate retention energy and budget disproportionately on converting 1× buyers. Once someone buys twice, the natural loyalty trajectory largely takes over.
🇮🇪
Ireland's advantage is greatest at the 1→2 step
Ireland converts 23.4% of first-time buyers to a second purchase vs 13.9% for UK — a 68% higher rate. But by the 3→4 step the gap narrows significantly (35.9% vs 29.1%). Ireland's advantage is in acquiring higher-quality customers who are more likely to make that critical first repeat. Once in the loyalty loop, both markets behave more similarly.
💡
Your VIP customers need minimal intervention
The 8,354 customers who've bought 3+ times have a 34–52% chance of buying again with very little prompting — they just need to be kept warm with new arrivals and stock updates. Don't invest heavy win-back resource here. Save the incentive budget for the 135,540 people at step 1→2.

🏆
VIP segment needs a dedicated programme immediately
8,354 customers have purchased 3 or more times and generate avg LTV of £359–674. They currently receive the same treatment as everyone else. This is your most commercially valuable segment and it has no programme built around it.
🏠
Identify multi-room buyers and prioritise for cross-sell
Any customer with underlay on their second order should be flagged as a multi-room buyer. They've already shown they're furnishing multiple spaces — content helping them complete each room is the most relevant thing you can show them.
📦
Use purchase transitions to remove guesswork from cross-sell
The transition data tells you exactly what to show. Zoe buyers respond to Soft Washable Vintage. Jute buyers respond to Renuu Zoe. This is more effective than generic new arrivals content and requires no additional research — the data is already here.

The most time-sensitive action

Your Q4 2025 BFCM cohort — 38,806 customers, your largest ever — is at peak repeat purchase probability right now. April and May 2026 are the highest-probability months for a second purchase from this cohort. Any retention activity landing on them in the next 4–6 weeks operates at maximum effectiveness. After day 90, you're in win-back territory, which is structurally harder and more expensive to convert.
